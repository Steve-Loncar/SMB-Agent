## RULES

1) ONE HERO IMAGE — ONE SCENE
- Your poster_prompt must describe a photographic scene consistent with the hero image: same subject category, same photographic style, same mood and colour palette.
- If you can see the attached image, describe its actual content (lighting, textures, colours, composition) — do not guess or generalise.
- Do NOT invent a different scene. If the image is a close-up of aged beef, the prompt is about aged beef.
- Do NOT describe a generic or unrelated background (no beaches, no abstract colours, no stock cliches).

2) DESCRIBE WHAT TO PRODUCE — NOT WHAT EXISTS
Write the prompt as a creative direction brief. Be specific:
- Subject: what is the focal object/person/scene
- Lighting: direction, quality (warm/cool, hard/soft, natural/studio)
- Composition: where the subject sits in the frame, where negative space exists for text
- Colour palette: derived from brand_colors + the tone of the image
- Photography style: photorealistic, editorial food photography, craft documentary, etc.

3) TEXT ON THE POSTER (MANDATORY)
The poster MUST include typographic text rendered directly in the image:
- HEADLINE: largest text element, bold, dominant position in the negative space zone
- SUPPORTING COPY (if provided): smaller, positioned near headline
- CTA (if provided): compact, distinct zone (bottom strip or corner)
- Spell every word EXACTLY as provided in the concept — do not paraphrase, abbreviate, or rephrase
- Typography style: clean, bold, modern sans-serif (like Helvetica, DIN, or Futura weight)
- Text colour MUST contrast strongly against the background (white on dark areas, dark on light areas)
- Text must be legible at a glance — OOH viewers have 3-6 seconds

4) gpt-IMAGE-1 PROMPT STYLE
- Open with: "Professional UK OOH poster."
- Describe the photographic background scene first
- Then specify the exact text to render, in quotes, with placement and style
- Write in plain English, production-directed, as if briefing a designer
- Do NOT write "flat graphic design poster layout" — this produces label/vintage slop
- Do NOT reference AI model names in the prompt
- End with: "Clean professional sans-serif typography. No decorative or script fonts. No AI artefacts."

5) UK OOH FORMAT RULES
- 6-sheet portrait (1024x1536): foot traffic, close viewing — bold subject, clean zone for headline
- 48-sheet landscape (1536x1024): roadside motorists — VERY simple, one massive image, minimal copy
- ~40% of the poster area should be usable for text (negative space, dark overlay zone, or tinted band)
- ONE dominant focal subject — no collages, no multi-subject scenes

6) BRAND CONSISTENCY + BUSINESS SPECIFICITY
Use guidelines.brand_colors, brand_fonts, brand_motifs to inform:
- Background tone and palette
- Text colour choices (use brand primary/accent where appropriate)
- Depth and texture feel (rustic craft vs. clean modern)
- Lighting warmth
- If brand_colors are empty, derive appropriate colours from the hero image mood and vertical

Use business_summary to make the scene SPECIFIC to this business — not a generic version of its category:
- key_offers → name the actual product/service in the scene. "dry-aged ribeye steak" not "meat". "flat white coffee" not "drink".
- key_proof_points → use as visual texture cues. "28-day dry-aged" → crusty aged fat cap. "handmade daily" → visible craft process detail.
- tone → map to photographic mood: "premium" = dark moody studio light; "warm craft" = golden natural light; "energetic" = high contrast dynamic; "minimal" = clean white background.
- target_customer → influence the human presence / scale / setting if people appear.
